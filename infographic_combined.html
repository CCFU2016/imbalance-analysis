Key Takeaways

  • A baseload 1 MW asset earned between 581k and 2,058k EUR annually — averaging 987k EUR/year. A 14-day negative return has never occurred in 5 years.
  • A solar asset earns 47–58% less than baseload — worse than the ~51% reduction in operating hours, because daytime imbalance prices tend to be depressed by solar generation on the grid.
  • Solar negative return probabilities are roughly 2–3x higher across all windows. A 24h loss occurs up to 18.9% of the time (solar, 2024) vs 10.1% (baseload).
  • For solar, the 14-day window is no longer risk-free — in 2024 there was a 1.4% chance of a negative 14-day return, a scenario that never occurred with baseload.
  • Maximum single-period losses are comparable (-5,699 solar vs -5,279 baseload in 2024), but represent a much larger share of solar's reduced annual revenue.
  • 2024 was the riskiest year by every measure for both asset types. 2022 was the most profitable and least risky.
