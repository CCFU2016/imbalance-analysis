Solar Asset Scenario

1 MW solar asset producing during daylight hours on the Dutch imbalance market | 2021–2025

Assumptions

  • Location: Netherlands (52.1°N, 5.3°E)
  • Constant output of 1 MW whenever the sun is up
  • Sunrise/sunset calculated using NOAA solar algorithm per day
  • An interval counts as "daylight" if its midpoint falls between sunrise and sunset
  • No cloud cover, inverter losses, or curtailment modelled — this is a theoretical maximum
  • Daylight ranges from ~7.8h (Dec) to ~16.7h (Jun), averaging ~51% of all intervals

Key Takeaways — Solar vs Baseload

  • Solar revenue is 47–58% lower than baseload — worse than the ~51% reduction in operating hours because daytime imbalance prices tend to be lower (more solar on the grid).
  • Negative return probabilities are significantly higher for solar: a 24h loss occurs up to 18.9% of the time (2024) vs 10.1% for baseload.
  • The 14-day window is no longer risk-free — in 2024 there was a 1.4% chance of a negative 14-day period, a scenario that never occurred with baseload.
  • Max single-period losses are similar in magnitude (-5,699 vs -5,279 EUR in 2024), but represent a larger share of the reduced annual revenue.
  • 2024 stands out as particularly challenging for solar: nearly 1 in 5 days had a negative return, and even 7-day windows were negative 6.1% of the time.
