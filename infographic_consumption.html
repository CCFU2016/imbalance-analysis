Key Takeaways

  • Annual consumption cost at imbalance prices ranged from 581k to 2,058k EUR (flat) and 691k to 2,254k EUR (residential) over 5 years.
  • The residential profile pays 10–19% more than flat consumption. Residential demand peaks align with higher-priced evening hours, pushing up the effective average price from ~66–235 to ~75–245 EUR/MWh.
  • The exception is 2022: the energy crisis drove extreme prices during off-peak/industrial hours, so residential worst-case costs were actually slightly lower than flat for 72h, 7d, and 14d windows.
  • Negative-cost days (where you'd be paid to consume) occur 1–10% of the time on a 24h basis. The profiles are nearly identical here — the earning opportunity depends on when negative prices occur, not consumption shape.
  • 2024 offered the most earning opportunities (10% of days) and the lowest average price — the best year for consumers. 2022 was the most expensive by far, with worst 7-day costs exceeding 100k EUR.
